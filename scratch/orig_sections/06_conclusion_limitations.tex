\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

Function calling does not always need autoregressive generation. For turns whose arguments can be grounded in the request, the dialogue, or the schema, \textsc{SpanCall} offers a fast alternative: a 395M-parameter anchor-conditioned encoder with a tool-selection head ($H_1$), a type-pre-filtered boundary-factorized span pointer network ($H_3$), and an exclusivity-constrained DFS decoder. Trained on a 1:1 corpus-balanced mix of Hermes V1 and xLAM-60k (117,760 instances) with macro-averaged dev selection, it reaches 0.874 macro dev exact call (0.882 peak on a disjoint probe), \textbf{0.743} \texttt{exact\_F} on Hermes eval and \textbf{0.766} on xLAM eval (\textbf{0.755} macro average), and \textbf{0.711--0.799} zero-shot across 14,423 full unseen-schema and paraphrase calls.

\paragraph{Where the approach stops---and why routing works.}
The 1:1 matched multi-LLM comparison makes the division of labour explicit. On all-scalar (\textsc{Normal}) calls ($n_F{=}1{,}108$ per corpus), \textsc{SpanCall} beats both \texttt{google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it} and \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-120b} on both corpora (\textbf{0.798} vs.\ 0.360--0.397 on Hermes; \textbf{0.840} vs.\ 0.755 on xLAM) at $38.8\times$--$91.3\times$ lower amortised mean latency. On \textsc{Complex} (\texttt{array}/\texttt{object}) calls ($n_F{=}169$ per corpus), \textsc{SpanCall} drops to 0.278--0.385 while both LLMs reach 0.568--0.586 on Hermes and \textbf{0.923} on xLAM, because single-span extraction over a linear candidate pool cannot represent multi-span array cardinality or nested leaf grouping. Deployed as an abstention-gated fast path (\textbf{Router}), \textsc{SpanCall} reaches 0.807--0.808 macro \texttt{exact\_F}---invoking the LLM on only \textbf{17.6\%} of turns, saving \textbf{79.6\%--80.1\%} of tokens and cutting mean latency by \textbf{$4.5\times$--$5.2\times$} (median 56.9\,ms). Adding a deterministic post-$H_1$ schema-type gate that routes \texttt{array}/\texttt{object} tools directly to the LLM (\textbf{Router+Schema}) recovers 100\% of the LLM's complex-call accuracy while preserving \textsc{SpanCall}'s scalar advantage, pushing macro \texttt{exact\_F} to \textbf{0.855--0.857} (0.821--0.825 Hermes, \textbf{0.889} xLAM; +11.1--11.2\,pp over both LLMs on xLAM) at \textbf{34.3\%} LLM calls, \textbf{59.7\%--59.9\%} token savings, and $2.6\times$--$2.7\times$ lower mean latency (median 66.4\,ms). Dedicated Stratum~A abstention training and multi-turn agentic trajectory evaluation are the two highest-leverage next steps.

\section*{Limitations}

We flag five limits of our evaluation. \textbf{Single training seed.} Although we report 10,000-resample paired bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over the 1,277 Stratum~F instances per corpus, \textsc{SpanCall} is trained with one random seed (42), so training-run variance is not measured. \textbf{Zero-shot LLM baselines and API rate limits.} Both \texttt{google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it} and \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-120b} are evaluated zero-shot with a no-decline prompt rather than fine-tuned on Hermes/xLAM, so their \texttt{false\_hit\_on\_A} is prompt-dependent and we do not separate extraction from in-domain adaptation. In addition, hosted API rate limits (\texttt{HTTP 429}) caused errors on 4.3\%--5.8\% of \texttt{gemma-4-26b} turns and 1.9\%--2.2\% of \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} turns (scored as misses); even under a worst-case assumption that every errored call would have been an exact match, \textbf{Router+Schema} still outperforms both LLMs on every corpus. \textbf{Latency comparability.} \textsc{SpanCall}'s latency is amortised over batch size 8 on a local GPU, whereas LLM latency is a hosted network round trip; we report no single-request CPU latency. \textbf{Abstention.} Semantic abstention on Stratum~A remains weak (\texttt{false\_hit\_on\_A} of 0.638--0.690 on Tier~1 and 0.788--0.816 on post-sanitization adversarial turns), and the Router inherits the fallback LLM's high false-hit rate on abstained turns. \textbf{Scope.} Evaluation covers single-call turns from Hermes V1 and xLAM-60k (with schema-disjoint and paraphrase OOD splits) rather than external multi-turn benchmarks such as BFCL \citep{yan2024bfcl} or $\tau$-bench \citep{yao2025taubench}.
